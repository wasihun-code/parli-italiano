## Ideal Learning Flow Inside Each Micro‑Lesson

This design describes **how** the platform should guide a learner through new words, phrases, sentences, and conversation turns – without generating actual content. All exercise types are from the provided list.

---

## 1. Word Learning Flow

**Goal:** Move a word from UNSEEN to MASTERED, focusing on recognition, then recall, then production.

| Step | Exercise Type | What the learner does | Purpose | Mastery required to advance |
|------|---------------|----------------------|---------|----------------------------|
| **1 – Introduction** | **Listen** | Hear the word (isolated, then in a simple carrier phrase). | First exposure; auditory encoding. | 1 successful listen (automatically passes). |
| **2 – Recognition** | **ListenChoose** | Hear the word and select the correct translation from 3‑4 written options (in L1 or L2). | Map sound to meaning. | 2 correct out of 2 attempts. |
| **3 – Visual Recognition** | **Match** | Match written Italian word to English translation (drag & drop or tap pairs). | Reinforce orthography. | 100% correct on a set of 4 words (including this one). |
| **4 – Recall (active)** | **Recall** | See English prompt (or picture), type or say the Italian word. | Active retrieval from memory. | Correct on first attempt. If wrong, step repeats after brief review. |
| **5 – Production (spelling)** | **Spelling** | Hear the word, type it correctly. | Orthographic accuracy. | 100% correct on first attempt (if fails, retry once before marking as incomplete). |
| **6 – Maintenance** | **Review** (spaced) | Word reappears in later micro‑lessons as a fast **Match** or **Recall** exercise. | Prevent forgetting. | Not required for micro‑lesson completion; tracks long‑term retention. |

**Mastery gate to unlock phrase learning:**  
A word must be at least **PRACTICED** (Step 4 completed successfully) before it can be used in a new phrase inside the same micro‑lesson. In practice, for the first word of a micro‑lesson, the learner completes Steps 1–4 before the phrase stage begins.

---

## 2. Phrase Learning Flow

**Goal:** Combine known words into meaningful chunks. Phrases are introduced only after all dependent words are at least PRACTICED.

| Step | Exercise Type | What the learner does | Purpose | Mastery requirement |
|------|---------------|----------------------|---------|---------------------|
| **1 – Introduction** | **Listen** | Hear the full phrase at natural speed. | Auditory pattern recognition. | 1 successful listen. |
| **2 – Meaning mapping** | **ListenChoose** | Hear the phrase, choose correct English meaning. | Associate sound with meaning without reading. | 1 correct out of 1. |
| **3 – Recognition** | **Match** | Match written Italian phrase to English translation. | Reinforce written form. | 100% correct on set of 3‑5 phrases. |
| **4 – Production (building)** | **Assembly** | Given words in scrambled order (possibly with extra distractors), assemble the phrase correctly. | Internalise word order and grammatical agreements. | Correct on first attempt; optional retry. |
| **5 – Recall** | **Recall** | See English prompt, say or type the Italian phrase. | Active retrieval. | Correct on first attempt. |
| **6 – Pronunciation** | **Speaking** | Listen to the phrase, record own voice, compare to model (with optional feedback). | Oral fluency. | Successful recording (no scoring required for mastery, but encouraged). |

**Mastery gate to unlock sentence learning:**  
A phrase must be **PRACTICED** (Steps 1–5 completed) before it can be used in a new sentence. The speaking step is recommended but not mandatory for unlocking sentences.

---

## 3. Sentence Learning Flow

**Goal:** Use known words and phrases to form full communicative sentences. Each sentence is a real utterance from a conversation turn.

| Step | Exercise Type | What the learner does | Purpose | Mastery requirement |
|------|---------------|----------------------|---------|---------------------|
| **1 – Introduction** | **Listen** | Hear the sentence (host or user utterance) with clear intonation. | First exposure in conversational context. | 1 listen. |
| **2 – Comprehension** | **Reading** | Read the sentence (Italian) and a side‑by‑side English translation. | Connect written form to meaning. | Visual inspection (pass). |
| **3 – Listening for detail** | **ListenChoose** | Hear a gap in the sentence (e.g., missing word or phrase) and select the missing part. | Focus on specific linguistic features. | Correct on first try. |
| **4 – Reconstruction** | **BuildSentence** | Given words/phrases in scrambled order, place them in correct sequence. | Syntactic awareness. | Correct on first attempt. |
| **5 – Recall** | **Dictation** | Hear the sentence, write or type it exactly. | Accurate reproduction. | 100% correct (typing). If wrong, show correct answer and repeat with different dictation. |
| **6 – Oral production** | **Speaking** | Read the sentence aloud, recording voice. | Pronunciation and fluency. | Successful recording. |

**Mastery gate to unlock conversation turn:**  
A sentence must be **PRACTICED** (Steps 1–5 completed) before its corresponding conversation turn is introduced. Step 6 (Speaking) is recommended for later review.

---

## 4. Conversation Turn Flow

**Goal:** Use sentences in a simulated two‑person exchange. Each turn is a **single** speaker utterance (host or user). The learner practices **both roles** progressively.

| Step | Exercise Type | What the learner does | Purpose | Mastery requirement |
|------|---------------|----------------------|---------|---------------------|
| **1 – Passive comprehension** | **Listen** | Hear the turn alone (e.g., host question). | Familiarisation. | 1 listen. |
| **2 – Active listening** | **ListenChoose** | Hear the turn, then choose the correct next line from 2‑3 options. | Understanding conversational function. | Correct on first attempt. |
| **3 – Role‑play (host side)** | **Speaking** | Read the host’s line aloud (text visible). | Practice delivery. | Successful recording. |
| **4 – Role‑play (user side)** | **Recall** + **Speaking** | Given context (e.g., “host just said X”), produce the user’s line without text. | Unprompted production. | Recorded and accepted (self‑assessment or AI check). |
| **5 – Full turn pair** | **Conversation** | Platform speaks one line, learner responds with the appropriate line (both roles cycled). | Realistic turn‑taking. | Successful completion of 2 exchanges (learner as both host and user). |

After mastering **two consecutive turns** (e.g., host turn + user turn), the learner unlocks the next micro‑lesson’s turns.

---

## 5. Review System

**Goal:** Maintain long‑term retention without re‑teaching. The system distinguishes **Introduction** from **Review** using timestamps and performance data.

### 5.1 When to Review

| Item Type | First review | Subsequent reviews (spaced repetition) |
|-----------|--------------|----------------------------------------|
| Word | After 1 day | 3 days, 7 days, 14 days, 30 days |
| Phrase | After 2 days | 4 days, 8 days, 16 days, 31 days |
| Sentence | After 3 days | 5 days, 10 days, 20 days, 40 days |
| Turn | After 4 days | 6 days, 12 days, 24 days, 48 days |

If a learner **fails** a review item, it resets to PRACTICED and reappears the next day.

### 5.2 Review Exercise Types

| Item | Review exercise (randomly selected from set) |
|------|------------------------------------------------|
| Word | Match (Italian–English) or Recall (English prompt) or Spelling |
| Phrase | ListenChoose or Assembly |
| Sentence | Dictation or BuildSentence |
| Turn | Conversation (short 1‑exchange) |

Reviews are **injected** into the micro‑lesson flow after the new content but before the final conversation practice. Typically **2–4 review items** per micro‑lesson.

### 5.3 Distinguishing Introduction vs Review

The system tracks each item’s **state**:

- **UNSEEN** → never appeared.
- **INTRODUCED** → has been presented in a micro‑lesson (Steps 1‑2 of its flow) but not yet mastered.
- **PRACTICED** → completed all mastery steps except long‑term maintenance.
- **MASTERED** → passed the first review and all subsequent spaced reviews.

**Rule:** An item in PRACTICED or MASTERED state will **never** receive introductory exercises again. Instead, it receives Review exercises with shorter, faster interactions (e.g., a single Match instead of full Listen+Match+Recall).

---

## 6. Mastery Gates

These are the **score or evidence requirements** that unlock higher levels.

| From | To | Requirement |
|------|----|--------------|
| Word → Phrase | The word must be in **PRACTICED** state (Step 4 of word flow completed). |
| Phrase → Sentence | The phrase must be in **PRACTICED** state (Steps 1‑5 of phrase flow completed). |
| Sentence → Turn | The sentence must be in **PRACTICED** state (Steps 1‑5 of sentence flow completed). |
| Turn → Next micro‑lesson | Each turn in the micro‑lesson must be **PRACTICED** (Steps 1‑4 of turn flow). For a micro‑lesson with 3 turns, all 3 must be practiced before moving on. |
| Micro‑lesson → Next micro‑lesson | No hard gate; learners may advance if they have **practiced** all turns, but **mastery** (passing reviews) is encouraged before moving far ahead. |

**Optional “Mastery Challenge”:** After completing a micro‑lesson, a short cumulative test (using Review exercises) must be passed with 80% to unlock the next micro‑lesson. This prevents learners from skipping reviews.

---

## 7. Complete Micro‑Lesson Flow

A typical micro‑lesson teaching **3 new words + 2 new phrases + 2 new sentences + 2 conversation turns** proceeds as follows:

### Stage 1 – Word Introduction (10‑15 min)
- For each new word (in order of dependency):
  1. Listen
  2. ListenChoose
  3. Match (with other new words)
  4. Recall
  5. Spelling

### Stage 2 – Phrase Construction (5‑8 min)
- For each new phrase:
  1. Listen
  2. ListenChoose (meaning)
  3. Match (Italian ↔ English)
  4. Assembly (scrambled words)
  5. Recall (English prompt → Italian phrase)

### Stage 3 – Sentence Formation (8‑10 min)
- For each new sentence:
  1. Listen
  2. Reading (show text + translation)
  3. ListenChoose (gap fill)
  4. BuildSentence (scrambled)
  5. Dictation (write the whole sentence)

### Stage 4 – Review Injection (3‑5 min)
- Randomly select 2‑4 items (words, phrases, sentences) from previous micro‑lessons that are due for review.
- Present each as a quick Match, ListenChoose, or Recall.

### Stage 5 – Conversation Turn Preparation (5‑7 min)
- For each new turn:
  1. Listen to the turn alone.
  2. ListenChoose (choose correct response).
  3. Speaking (read the turn aloud – if host turn, read host line; if user turn, read user line).
  4. Recall (without text, produce the user response to a host prompt).

### Stage 6 – Conversation Practice (3‑5 min)
- Using the **Conversation** exercise:
  - Platform speaks one turn, learner speaks the next.
  - Cycle through both roles.
  - If mistakes occur, repeat the pair once.

### Stage 7 – Micro‑Lesson Completion
- Brief summary showing progress.
- Option to repeat weak items.
- Unlock next micro‑lesson.

---

## 8. Rationale For Every Stage

| Stage | Why it exists |
|-------|----------------|
| **Word Introduction** | Words are atomic. Without solid recognition and recall, phrases and sentences become guesswork. The sequence (listen → recognition → match → recall → spelling) ensures multi‑modal encoding. |
| **Phrase Construction** | Phrases are the natural bridge. Assembly forces the learner to respect word order and agreement without memorising by rote. |
| **Sentence Formation** | Sentences are the unit of conversation. Dictation and BuildSentence ensure syntactic completeness. |
| **Review Injection** | Spaced repetition is essential for long‑term memory. Placing reviews **before** conversation practice prevents forgetting and reinforces prior content. |
| **Conversation Turn Preparation** | Turns require pragmatic awareness (e.g., knowing which response fits). ListenChoose trains comprehension; Recall without text trains spontaneous production. |
| **Conversation Practice** | The final simulation mimics real interaction. Cycling both roles builds empathy and prepares learners for unpredictable turns. |

**Cognitive load management:** Each stage has a clear, limited set of exercises. Learners never see more than 3 new items of a type before practicing them. The flow from recognition → recall → production is explicit and repeatable.

---

This design ensures that every item is introduced once, practiced thoroughly, and reviewed intelligently – preparing learners to achieve **conversation mastery** without frustration.
